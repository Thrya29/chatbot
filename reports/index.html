Handoff Lab: results

An AI-assistant-to-human handoff for a regulated trading platform's support chat, built end to end against mocks, then measured honestly. All data is synthetic. Generated by make report.

1. The headline: keyword rules don't generalise

Rules v1 were tuned on 40 dev conversations and then scored on a held-out set written beforehand: they reached a person in 37% of the cases that needed one. Rules v2 were rewritten by concept after reading those misses, so the v1 set is no longer blind (grey). A second held-out set was written after v2 was frozen. On it, v2 reaches 43%. Blue bars are the honest numbers. Error bars are 95% Wilson intervals.

What this means for the design. The rules stay, because they are fast, auditable and cannot be prompt-injected. But they are a floor, not a detector. The model layer (llm_detector.py) can only add flags, so a wrong or manipulated model can at worst send someone to a person. It is scored with make eval-llm when an API key is present. It hasn't been run in this repository, so no number is claimed for it. Until a model layer clears the self-harm and fraud gates on blind data, a visible "talk to a person" option on every turn is the safety net.

2. Containment is not resolution

The simulated month is calibrated to the data pack (5,000 chats, 38% contained). The brief says 12% of contained chats come back within 48 hours. Counting those as resolved overstates the AI by about four points of all chats.

3. The survey flatters both cohorts

About 9% of AI-resolved and 22% of handed-off clients answer. Clients who abandon the queue almost never answer: 6 of 548 in this simulation. So the dashboard reads high. The orange bars use every chat's (simulated) score. The gap is the part of client experience that CSAT can't see, which is why re-contact and abandonment are tracked alongside it as behavioural metrics.

4. Mix shift: handed-off CSAT can fall when nothing got worse

Suppose containment rises 10 points on the four easiest topics (KYC, Platform, Statements, Product info), and no agent changes anything. Overall containment goes from 37.9% to 42.5%. The chats left for humans are now harder, so handed-off CSAT moves from 3.31 to 3.28 (-0.033): -0.033 from mix and +0.000 from performance. Without the decomposition, that dip would be blamed on the handoff.

5. How long before a CSAT change is even measurable?

Survey responses needed per arm to detect a change at 95% confidence with 80% power (SD ≈ 1.15 from the simulation), compared with the responses one month produces at today's volumes.

Change to detectResponses per armMonths to collect
0.1 points2,074AI-resolved: 12.1 months, Handed off: 3.0 months, One topic's handoffs (Funding): 14.0 months
0.2 points519AI-resolved: 3.0 months, Handed off: 0.8 months, One topic's handoffs (Funding): 3.5 months
0.3 points231AI-resolved: 1.4 months, Handed off: 0.3 months, One topic's handoffs (Funding): 1.6 months

A 0.3-point move in handed-off CSAT is readable within a month. The same move for one topic's handoffs takes about two months. A 0.1-point move takes a quarter across all handoffs and about a year for AI-resolved chats. So the primary success metrics are behavioural: re-ask rate, re-contact, abandonment, time to first human reply. CSAT is a guardrail that must not fall.
